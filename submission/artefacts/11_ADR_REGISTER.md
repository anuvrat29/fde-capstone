# 11 Adr Register

## Purpose

Meaningful Architecture Decision Records with options, trade-offs, consequences and review triggers, formalising the 12+ design decisions already made across artefacts 01–10 rather than inventing new, untested decisions.

## Acceptance criteria

- The artefact is evidence-led and references supplied paths, record locators and inject IDs.
- Facts, assumptions, inference, conflict, decision and residual risk are distinguishable.
- Temporal, jurisdictional, authorization and authority boundaries are explicit where relevant.
- Owners, approvals, review triggers and measurable acceptance gates are named.
- The artefact contains no unsupported coverage, pricing, reserve, payment or treaty conclusion.

## Artefact-specific acceptance criteria

- Every ADR cites the artefact where the decision was first made; this register does not introduce a decision that was not already reasoned through in 01–10 (CTRL-ADR-01).
- Every ADR names at least one rejected option with a stated reason, not merely the selected option (CTRL-ADR-02).
- Every ADR has a review trigger that is either a date, a metric threshold, or a named event — never "ongoing" alone (CTRL-ADR-03).
- At least 12 ADRs are recorded, per DEFINITION_OF_DONE.md's "at least 12 meaningful ADRs" requirement (CTRL-ADR-04).

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Option | Benefits | Risks | Cost/complexity | Reversibility | Decision and rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Write brand-new ADRs from scratch for this register, independent of the reasoning already captured in artefacts 01–10 | Could produce a cleaner, more uniform ADR narrative style | Risks contradicting or duplicating decisions already reasoned through and evidenced in 01–10; would fail CTRL-ADR-01 and create two competing decision records for the same choice | Higher effort; duplicates work already done | Reversible but wasteful | Rejected. Every ADR above cites its originating artefact and reuses that artefact's own Alternatives and trade-offs analysis rather than re-deriving it. |
| Register only the architecture-specific decisions (fraud-ring graph, system-of-record write boundary) and leave earlier business/domain decisions undocumented as ADRs | Smaller register, faster to produce | Would likely fall short of the 12-ADR minimum in DEFINITION_OF_DONE.md and would omit genuinely architecture-relevant decisions made earlier (e.g., wording-version modelling, which directly shapes the Container/Component data model) | Lower effort | Reversible | Rejected. The 12-ADR minimum requires consolidating the full decision set, and several "earlier" decisions (ADR-05 through ADR-09) are architecturally load-bearing, not merely business decisions. |
| Consolidate 13 decisions spanning business, domain, data-governance, graph and architecture scope into one register, each cross-referenced to its originating artefact | Meets the 12-ADR minimum with genuinely distinct, evidenced decisions; avoids duplicating or contradicting prior artefacts | Requires keeping this register synchronised if any originating artefact's decision is later revised | Medium — one-time consolidation, ongoing synchronisation discipline | Reversible; any ADR can be superseded by a new one citing this register | Selected. Satisfies CTRL-ADR-04 with 13 ADRs, each independently meaningful per A-01, cross-referenced without renumbering existing IDs (A-02). |
