# 30 Final Defence

## Purpose

Executive, product, architecture, evaluation, security, economics and handover defence, mapping the 25-minute session structure in requirements/FINAL_DEFENCE.md to the specific claims, evidence and challenge questions this submission must be able to answer live.

## Acceptance criteria

- The artefact is evidence-led and references supplied paths, record locators and inject IDs.
- Facts, assumptions, inference, conflict, decision and residual risk are distinguishable.
- Temporal, jurisdictional, authorization and authority boundaries are explicit where relevant.
- Owners, approvals, review triggers and measurable acceptance gates are named.
- The artefact contains no unsupported coverage, pricing, reserve, payment or treaty conclusion.

## Artefact-specific acceptance criteria

- Every one of the 7 defence segments in requirements/FINAL_DEFENCE.md has at least one claim-to-defend row naming the specific artefact/control that backs it, not a generic restatement (CTRL-FD-01).
- Every claim's challenge question is a question a panel could plausibly ask, not a softball the team already knows the answer to (CTRL-FD-02).
- The team's answer to "what is the system not authorized to decide" (per requirements/FINAL_DEFENCE.md's closing instruction) is stated explicitly and completely, listing all ten regulated actions from 14_INSURANCE_CONTROL_BOUNDARIES.md (CTRL-FD-03).
- No claim in this artefact asserts a stronger state than 21_ASSURANCE_CASE.md and 28_PRODUCTION_READINESS.md already established; this artefact prepares the defence of existing claims, it does not inflate them (CTRL-FD-04).

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Option | Benefits | Risks | Cost/complexity | Reversibility | Decision and rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare polished, rehearsed answers for a predicted set of likely panel questions, optimising for a smooth-sounding delivery | Could produce a more confident-sounding session | Per A-01, the panel may select any disclosed inject/scenario/role/endpoint; a rehearsed-answer-only strategy fails immediately on any unanticipated selection, and risks sounding evasive if a challenge question falls outside the rehearsed set | Lower preparation depth (fewer artefacts need be internalised), high risk of a visible gap under an unexpected question | Reversible before the session, not during it | Rejected. A-01 requires preparing the retrieval method (which artefact, which evidence ID), not a fixed script. |
| Inflate claim strength during the defence to sound more complete than 21/28 already established, treating the live session as an opportunity to "round up" | Might create a more impressive first impression | Directly contradicts A-04 and the honest claim-strength discipline maintained since Step 22; a panel cross-checking any inflated claim against the submitted artefacts would find the discrepancy immediately, which is far more damaging than the honest gap itself | No preparation cost, catastrophic credibility risk if caught (near-certain, since the panel has the same artefacts) | N/A — cannot be walked back mid-session | Rejected outright. |
| Segment-by-segment claim-to-defend preparation naming the specific backing artefact/control for each, honest challenge questions (not softballs), a complete ten-action closing answer, and strict adherence to already-established claim strength (never inflated) | Prepares the team to answer any panel-selected question by retrieval method rather than memorisation; honestly represents the submission's actual state including its largest exposure (zero implementation) | Requires the team to be comfortable stating "not yet implemented" or "not yet executed" repeatedly during the live session, which is less comfortable than a polished narrative | Medium — one preparation artefact covering all 7 segments plus the closing question | Reversible; can be refined up to the session itself | Selected. This is the only approach consistent with CTRL-FD-01 through CTRL-FD-04 and the discipline this entire 27-artefact submission has maintained. |
