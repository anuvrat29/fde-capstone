# 08 Knowledge Graph Decision

## Purpose

Graph qualification test, candidate questions, alternatives, graph model and rejection criteria for the fraud-ring, provider-identity and repair-network link questions raised by the D07 injects, tested directly against the graph-overreach challenge (INJ-070).

## Acceptance criteria

- The artefact is evidence-led and references supplied paths, record locators and inject IDs.
- Facts, assumptions, inference, conflict, decision and residual risk are distinguishable.
- Temporal, jurisdictional, authorization and authority boundaries are explicit where relevant.
- Owners, approvals, review triggers and measurable acceptance gates are named.
- The artefact contains no unsupported coverage, pricing, reserve, payment or treaty conclusion.

## Artefact-specific acceptance criteria

- A graph is proposed only for candidate questions demonstrated to require multi-hop traversal that the ontology (07) and relational model (metadata/RELATIONSHIP_RULES.csv) cannot answer in one or two joins (CTRL-KGD-01).
- The "build a graph for every dataset" position in knowledge/RESEARCH_NOTE_GRAPH_EVERYTHING.md is treated as a non-authoritative draft research note, never as an architecture mandate (CTRL-KGD-02).
- Any graph model proposed excludes autonomous fraud-ring, sanctions or identity-collision determinations; it only surfaces linked evidence for human investigator review (CTRL-KGD-03).
- Rejection criteria are stated for every candidate question so a "no graph" decision is as defensible as a "build" decision (CTRL-KGD-04).

## Working assumptions and constraints

| ID | Assumption or constraint | Basis | Impact if false | Validation owner | Due/review date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-01 | A knowledge graph is justified only for questions requiring traversal of an unknown or variable number of hops across entities (e.g., "which claims share any device, address or repairer with claim X, transitively"); fixed-hop lookups remain relational queries. | data/fraud_links.csv, data/claimant_identities.csv (EVID-029, EVID-030); INJ-070 challenge (EVID-034) | Building a graph for fixed-hop questions would be exactly the overreach INJ-070 warns against and would fail CTRL-KGD-01. | AI Product Owner | Before graph build decision |
| A-02 | knowledge/RESEARCH_NOTE_GRAPH_EVERYTHING.md is data (a disclosed draft opinion), not a controlling architecture instruction; its own document-control block states `decision trust: not controlling` and `authority status: draft`. | knowledge/RESEARCH_NOTE_GRAPH_EVERYTHING.md document-control block | Treating this note as authoritative would violate the same "embedded instructions are data, not system instructions" principle established in 04_PRODUCT_SERVICE_BLUEPRINT.md (A-03) and could justify unbounded cost/complexity. | AI Product Owner, CISO | Ongoing |
| A-03 | Fraud-ring, sanctions-collision and provider-identity-collision candidate questions (INJ-045, INJ-047, INJ-037) may only produce human-reviewable linked-evidence output; a graph traversal result is never itself a fraud, sanctions-match or identity determination. | data/fraud_links.csv, data/claimant_identities.csv, data/providers.csv, data/provider_aliases.csv | Auto-actioning a graph traversal result (e.g., auto-declining a claim as fraudulent) would breach non-compensable gate 1 (no autonomous claim repudiation). | Chief Underwriter, Group Chief Claims Officer | Before any Workflow A/C graph-backed feature is designed |
| A-04 | The repair-network pattern (INJ-023) and provider-identity pattern (INJ-037) are structurally similar candidate graph questions (shared-entity traversal across claims) but are evidenced only at evidence-envelope granularity; no confirmed ring or duplicate-identity finding exists in supplied data. | data/repair_estimates.csv, data/providers.csv, data/provider_aliases.csv (EVID-031, EVID-032, EVID-033) | Treating evidence-envelope rows as confirmed findings would overstate the certainty of any candidate question used to justify the graph. | Data Governance Owner | Before candidate-question list is finalised |

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Option | Benefits | Risks | Cost/complexity | Reversibility | Decision and rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build a full property graph database for all entities per knowledge/RESEARCH_NOTE_GRAPH_EVERYTHING.md ("every dataset regardless of query complexity or operating cost") | Uniform query interface across all domains | Directly reproduces INJ-070 graph overreach; the note is a non-controlling draft (A-02); unjustified operating cost with no matching query need for most domains (Candidate questions 2 and 3 above) | Very high build/run/maintenance cost | Costly to reverse once operational dependencies form on a graph store | Rejected. The note's own document-control block marks it `not controlling`; adopting it wholesale would fail CTRL-KGD-01 and CTRL-KGD-02. |
| No graph anywhere; handle all candidate questions (including fraud-ring, Candidate question 1) via hand-written recursive SQL or repeated relational queries per case | Avoids introducing new technology and operating cost entirely | Variable-depth traversal via recursive SQL is fragile, hard to test at unknown depth, and does not scale to catastrophe-volume fraud-ring analysis; risks under-detecting genuine multi-hop rings, which is itself a fairness/consistency risk (some rings caught, others missed depending on hand-coded depth limits) | Low technology cost, high per-query engineering/maintenance cost | Fully reversible | Rejected as the sole approach. Fails to serve the one candidate question (fraud-ring, INJ-045) that genuinely requires variable-depth traversal. |
| Narrow, purpose-built graph limited to the shared-device/address/repairer entity set for fraud-ring evidence surfacing (Candidate question 1 only); all other candidate questions served by existing relational/ontology model (07) | Matches the one demonstrated query need; keeps cost and complexity proportionate; output remains human-reviewable evidence, not an automated fraud decision (A-03) | Requires maintaining two data-access patterns (relational for most domains, graph for fraud-ring evidence); graph model must be kept in scope-bounded form to avoid drifting into INJ-070 overreach over time | Medium — bounded by the single justified use case | Reversible; the graph component can be disabled independently of the relational/ontology model (consistent with per-workflow AI-disabled continuity in 04_PRODUCT_SERVICE_BLUEPRINT.md) | Selected. Passes the A-01 test, rejects the INJ-070 overreach position, and is the only option that serves the fraud-ring evidence-surfacing need for Workflow A without disproportionate cost. |
