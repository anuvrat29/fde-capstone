# 18 Responsible Ai Fairness

## Purpose

Fairness, explainability, subgroup performance, human factors, accessibility and appeals for the three workflows, closing the deeper remediation programme deferred from template 15's immediate use restrictions on PRC-MOTOR-9 and FRD-CLAIM-6, and addressing the confirmed multilingual quality gap, unexplainable adverse outcomes, vulnerable-customer handling and accessibility failures.

## Acceptance criteria

- The artefact is evidence-led and references supplied paths, record locators and inject IDs.
- Facts, assumptions, inference, conflict, decision and residual risk are distinguishable.
- Temporal, jurisdictional, authorization and authority boundaries are explicit where relevant.
- Owners, approvals, review triggers and measurable acceptance gates are named.
- The artefact contains no unsupported coverage, pricing, reserve, payment or treaty conclusion.

## Artefact-specific acceptance criteria

- PRC-MOTOR-9 and FRD-CLAIM-6's confirmed breaches (15_ACTUARIAL_MODEL_RISK.md) each receive a remediation pathway (retrain, feature removal, or retirement) with an owner and target date, not only the immediate disclosure recorded in template 15 (CTRL-RAF-01).
- Every material adverse-outcome-adjacent output names its controlling evidence and policy rule, per knowledge/FAIRNESS_AND_ADVERSE_ACTION.md, directly closing INJ-085 (CTRL-RAF-02).
- Vulnerable-customer indicators (INJ-063) route to a human-reviewed path, never generic automation, regardless of workflow (CTRL-RAF-03).
- Every accessibility failure pattern named in INJ-087 (colour-only severity, inaccessible PDFs, mouse-only controls) has a corresponding accessible-design requirement, per knowledge/ACCESSIBILITY_STANDARD.md (CTRL-RAF-04).

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Option | Benefits | Risks | Cost/complexity | Reversibility | Decision and rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Treat template 15's immediate disclosure as the final answer for PRC-MOTOR-9/FRD-CLAIM-6, with no further remediation pathway | Avoids additional design work now | Leaves a confirmed breach permanently unaddressed beyond disclosure, which is unlikely to satisfy a defence panel or a real regulator asking "what did you do about it, not just disclose it?" | Lowest cost, highest residual compliance/reputational risk | Reversible but the interim exposure window grows the longer remediation is deferred | Rejected. A-01 requires retrain/feature-removal/retirement as the deeper answer beyond disclosure. |
| Reconstruct explanations for adverse outcomes only after a complaint is received, to avoid building an at-output-time explanation mechanism | Lower upfront engineering cost | Directly reproduces the INJ-085 pattern; a reconstructed explanation may not accurately reflect what actually drove the original recommendation, undermining contestability | Lower cost now, unreliable and possibly misleading explanations later | Reversible in design, but any already-issued unexplainable recommendation cannot be retroactively fixed | Rejected. A-02 requires controlling-evidence and policy-rule citation at the point of output. |
| A single generic accessibility statement ("the system will be accessible") without pattern-specific requirements | Simplest to write | Does not demonstrably close any of the three specific INJ-087 patterns (colour-only, inaccessible PDF, mouse-only); an auditor or panel could easily show it unaddressed by testing any one pattern | Lowest documentation cost, high risk of failing an actual accessibility audit | Reversible but wastes the opportunity to specify testable requirements now | Rejected. | Remediation pathways for the two confirmed model breaches, at-output-time explanation citation, a cross-workflow vulnerability-routing rule, and three pattern-specific accessibility requirements — four targeted decisions, each reusing an existing control pattern where possible (e.g., the vulnerability rule extends rather than duplicates 04_PRODUCT_SERVICE_BLUEPRINT.md's persona-based routing) | Directly closes every fairness/explainability/accessibility inject examined in this pass with a testable, owned requirement | Four decisions to maintain and later evaluate (in template 22); requires Model Risk Owner and Customer Advocate coordination | Medium — four targeted decisions | Reversible; each can be tightened or loosened as more evidence arrives | Selected.
