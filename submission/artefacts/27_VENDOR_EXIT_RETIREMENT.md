# 27 Vendor Exit Retirement

## Purpose

Portability, model abstraction, data export, substitution, exit and retirement evidence for the vendor-dependent components of the three workflows, closing the Retirement lifecycle-stage gap explicitly identified in 20_ISO42001_GOVERNANCE.md and grounded in the confirmed vendor stack-concentration and capital-model-concentration injects.

## Acceptance criteria

- The artefact is evidence-led and references supplied paths, record locators and inject IDs.
- Facts, assumptions, inference, conflict, decision and residual risk are distinguishable.
- Temporal, jurisdictional, authorization and authority boundaries are explicit where relevant.
- Owners, approvals, review triggers and measurable acceptance gates are named.
- The artefact contains no unsupported coverage, pricing, reserve, payment or treaty conclusion.

## Artefact-specific acceptance criteria

- The confirmed stack-concentration finding (INJ-092: one vendor hosting model, vector store, OCR, evaluation, observability and key management) has an explicit portability/exit plan, not only a risk acknowledgement (CTRL-VE-01).
- The Retirement lifecycle-stage gap from 20_ISO42001_GOVERNANCE.md is formally closed here with a named control, satisfying that artefact's own deferral (CTRL-VE-02).
- Every data category a vendor holds has a stated export format and mechanism, consistent with data/data_licenses.csv's provenance concerns (INJ-017) (CTRL-VE-03).
- No exit/retirement plan assumes instantaneous vendor transition; every plan states a transition period consistent with data/continuity_requirements.csv's rto_hours values (CTRL-VE-04).

## Working assumptions and constraints

| ID | Assumption or constraint | Basis | Impact if false | Validation owner | Due/review date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-01 | The confirmed stack concentration (INJ-092, one vendor hosting model/vector-store/OCR/evaluation/observability/key-management) means a single vendor exit event would simultaneously affect six distinct capabilities, not one; the exit plan must therefore treat this as a correlated, not independent, risk — consistent with INJ-057's own framing of correlated exposures not represented in the internal model. | data/vendor_dependencies.csv (EVID-115) | Treating the six capabilities as independently substitutable would understate the actual exit complexity and could leave the organisation without a workable fallback for all six simultaneously. | AI Product Owner, CISO | Before any single-vendor consolidation decision |
| A-02 | Model portability is achieved by keeping every workflow's model call abstracted behind the model_registry.csv interface (model_id, purpose, approval_status) already established in 13_MODEL_PROMPT_LIFECYCLE.md, rather than hard-coding a specific vendor's API; this is the existing abstraction layer this artefact relies on, not a new one. | data/model_registry.csv (EVID-057); 13_MODEL_PROMPT_LIFECYCLE.md (reused) | Hard-coding a vendor-specific API into workflow logic would make model substitution (a required operating property per case/INTEGRATED_CASE.md Section 5, "model substitution... executable") far more costly to execute during an actual exit. | AI Product Owner | Ongoing |
| A-03 | Every data category a vendor holds (claim documents, evidence-reconciliation state, model training/evaluation data) must have an export mechanism that produces the data in a non-proprietary format (e.g., CSV, JSON) usable without that vendor's tooling, directly addressing the provenance concern raised by INJ-017 (unapproved external data with unclear provenance) by ensuring AMR's own data does not become similarly un-provenanced once exported. | data/data_licenses.csv (EVID-117) | A proprietary-format-only export would trade one provenance problem (INJ-017's unclear external data) for another (AMR's own data becoming unusable outside the exiting vendor's tooling). | Data Governance Owner | Before any vendor contract renewal is signed |
| A-04 | Transition periods for vendor exit must be at least as long as the corresponding capability's rto_hours value in data/continuity_requirements.csv (4/8/12 hours for catastrophe_claim_intake/statutory_reporting/reinsurance_notice respectively), since a vendor exit that cannot be completed within the capability's own recovery-time objective would itself become an incident, not a planned transition. | data/continuity_requirements.csv (EVID-119, reused from 25/26) | Assuming an instantaneous or arbitrarily-short transition period would produce an exit plan that is not actually executable within the operational constraints already established for these capabilities. | AI Product Owner, Group Chief Claims Officer | Before any vendor exit is initiated |

## Alternatives and trade-offs

| Option | Benefits | Risks | Cost/complexity | Reversibility | Decision and rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Treat the six stack-concentrated capabilities (model, vector store, OCR, evaluation, observability, key management) as a single bundled exit unit, planning one combined fallback rather than six individual ones | Simpler plan to write | Obscures which specific capability failure would actually block recovery of the others (key management is the clear single point of failure, per A-01's analysis); a bundled plan cannot prioritise which capability to restore first | Lower planning effort, high risk of an unprioritised, unclear recovery sequence during an actual exit | Reversible but wastes the opportunity to identify the true critical path (key management) | Rejected. A-01 requires individual capability-level analysis to correctly identify key management as the highest-severity dependency. |
| Export vendor-held data only in that vendor's proprietary format, accepting the resulting lock-in as an unavoidable cost of using the vendor's tooling | Lower export-engineering effort now | Trades INJ-017's unclear-external-data-provenance problem for an equally problematic AMR-data-unusable-outside-vendor problem; contradicts the non-proprietary-export principle already implicit in this submission's own hash-verifiable evidence discipline | Lower cost now, high cost and risk at actual exit time | Reversible only by re-exporting later, at additional cost | Rejected. A-03 requires non-proprietary, hash-preserving export from the outset, not as an afterthought at exit time. |
| Assume vendor transitions can be completed instantaneously or within an arbitrarily short window, to present a more reassuring-sounding plan | Appears more resilient on paper | Not executable in practice; a transition plan that cannot actually be completed within its stated window is a false assurance, similar in kind to the "fully assured" claim-inflation risk already rejected in 21_ASSURANCE_CASE.md's A-02 | Lower planning rigor, high risk of the plan failing when actually needed | N/A — an unexecutable plan cannot be "reversed" into a working one without redoing the analysis | Rejected. A-04 requires transition periods grounded in the same rto_hours evidence already used for incident recovery (24/25/26). |
| Capability-level fallback analysis identifying key management as the critical path, model-registry-based abstraction reused from 13, non-proprietary hash-preserving data export, and rto_hours-grounded transition periods with CatVision flagged for priority planning | Directly closes the Retirement gap with an executable, evidence-grounded plan; reuses the model-abstraction layer already built rather than inventing a new one; correctly identifies the true single point of failure | Requires ongoing discipline to keep the model-registry abstraction from eroding into vendor-specific hard-coding over time | Medium — four decisions, each reusing an existing pattern | Reversible; the plan can be refined as vendor contracts are renewed or new dependencies are added | Selected. Satisfies CTRL-VE-01 through CTRL-VE-04 and gives 20_ISO42001_GOVERNANCE.md's Retirement gap a concrete closing control. |
